Imagine a robot in a manufacturing cell that picks and assembles parts made of aluminium profiles and brackets, using an RGB-D camera as its main sensor. Choose a commercially available RGB-D camera as your reference and build a small pipeline that generates labelled synthetic RGB-D images of such a scene with any simulator or renderer. Instead of using ideal depth, model the imperfections that this camera would realistically show on these parts, and explain which effects you included, which you left out, and why.


Using this pipeline, design and run one small experiment that shows whether your camera model changes the outcome of a downstream perception or grasping step. Please report quantitative results, including what did not work.


            
The whole assignment can be completed on your own laptop or PC with freely available software. You do not need to own the camera, since its datasheet and published information are sufficient, and no robot or other hardware is required. If you have access to additional hardware or computing resources, you are welcome to use them, but this is not expected. You may also use any tools, including AI assistants; please add a short note on how you used them, including one place where you disagreed with or corrected a tool’s suggestion.

Please summarize your approach, solution and results in a PDF report of at most 8 pages; this report is mandatory. Please also share your code (for example as a GitHub repository) so that we can run it, and feel free to add supporting material such as videos or sketches. We deliberately leave the approach open, because we are interested in how you tackle the problem, your technical reasoning and your judgement, so there is no single expected solution.
